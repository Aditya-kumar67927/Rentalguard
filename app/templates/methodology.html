About & methodology

How RentalGuard reaches a risk score, what data it uses, and where it can be wrong.

What the score means

The score (0–100) is the sum of points from transparent rule indicators (capped at 80) plus up to 20 points from a machine-learning model.

risk_score = min(80, Σ indicator points) + round(20 × ML probability)
  • Low 0–24
  • Moderate 25–49
  • High 50–74
  • Very high 75–100

It is a risk assessment, not a fraud verdict. Listings can be genuine and still unusual (a landlord offering a real discount), and a risky listing can look ordinary.

Price anomaly

Expected rent comes from comparable listings, looked up with a fallback hierarchy: locality + BHK → area + BHK → zone + BHK → all Mumbai + BHK. When the area is known we use the median rent per sq.ft × area. A gradient-boosted regression model provides a second estimate. The two are blended in log-space, trusting the peer group more when it is large.

z = ln(actual / expected) / σ_peer (σ = 1.4826 × MAD, robust) HIGH if z ≤ −2.5 or actual < 45% of expected MODERATE if z ≤ −1.5 or actual < 65% of expected

Text analysis

Curated patterns detect advance-payment requests, payment before viewing, "no viewing" claims, owners abroad, urgency, unusual payment channels and requests for OTPs. Negations ("no advance required") and routine phrases ("immediately available") are excluded. A TF-IDF character n-gram model compares each sentence to known risky messages to catch paraphrases and misspellings.

Duplicates

Descriptions are normalised, hashed for exact matches and indexed with MinHash locality-sensitive hashing. Candidates are ranked by TF-IDF cosine similarity. A similarity ≥ 90% is a possible duplicate; it adds risk only when the copy has a very different rent or is in a different zone.

Contacts

Phone numbers and e-mails are hashed with SHA-256 and never displayed. Brokers legitimately post many listings, so repetition only adds points when a contact spans many localities or has been reported.

Data

Reference listings: 13,541 Mumbai rental listings from 99acres.com collected on 16 July 2018, published on GitHub (baldsatan/mumbai-rental-data-analysis). After cleaning and removing reposts, 9,139 listings remain. The repository has no explicit licence; the data is used here for non-commercial, educational research only and is not redistributed.

Rents are 2018 prices. Current rents are generally higher, so today's listings may look slightly expensive relative to the reference data. Low-price flags are affected less, because they rely on large deviations.

Risk labels are synthetic. No openly licensed, labelled Indian rental-scam dataset was available, so the ML model is trained on a documented simulation. Its metrics show how well it recovers that simulation, not real-world fraud-detection accuracy.
